Katelyn Huynh

Neuroscience and Global & Community Health

I am a first-year student at the University of California, Riverside, double majoring in Neuroscience and Global & Community Health. I am passionate about understanding the complexities of the human brain, improving access to healthcare, and exploring the ways science and community-based initiatives can work together to improve individual and public health outcomes. Through my academic interests, clinical experience, and leadership roles, I hope to build a strong foundation for a future career centered on healthcare, service, and making a meaningful difference in the lives of others. My interest in healthcare has grown through my experience as a Patient Care Coordinator and Intern at East Bay Health, a nonprofit clinic serving uninsured patients. In this role, I help coordinate care for more than 60 patients and have the opportunity to shadow physicians, gaining insight into patient care and the challenges individuals face when accessing healthcare services. This experience has strengthened my interest in healthcare accessibility and reinforced the importance of compassionate, patient-centered care. It has also motivated me to explore how neuroscience and community health can complement one another in addressing both individual medical needs and broader health disparities.

In addition to my healthcare experience, I have developed strong leadership, organizational, and communication skills through my work with the Lafayette-Moraga Youth Association (LMYA). As a Program Manager, I help oversee volleyball programs serving more than 3,000 athletes, managing scheduling, payroll processing, staff onboarding, and coordination across dozens of teams. In addition to this, as a volleyball trainer and referee, I coach young athletes and help create a positive, structured environment for skill development and teamwork. These responsibilities have taught me how to manage competing priorities, collaborate with diverse groups of people, and lead effectively in fast-paced environments. My commitment to working with children and supporting individuals with different needs extends to my other experiences. As a Kids Academy Instructor and Event Team Member at Life Time Fitness, I supervise children of various ages, facilitate activities, and help maintain a safe and engaging environment. I have also worked as a student helper at A Hop, Skip, and A Jump Ahead, assisting with social skills groups and individual sessions for children with learning disabilities. Through the PALS Program, I supported a camper with Down syndrome during a week-long summer camp. These experiences have strengthened my patience, adaptability, and appreciation for individualized support and inclusive environments.

Service and community involvement have also been central to my personal development. As Co-President of Miramonte Interact Club, I lead weekly meetings for an 80-member organization and help coordinate major community service events. My involvement has reinforced my belief in the importance of community engagement, collaboration, and using leadership to create positive change. During high school, I also participated in varsity volleyball and track and field, earning recognition as an NCS Scholar Athlete. Balancing athletics, academics, employment, and service taught me the value of discipline, perseverance, and time management. As I continue my studies at UC Riverside, I look forward to expanding my understanding of neuroscience and global & community health while pursuing opportunities to learn from healthcare professionals, contribute to research and community initiatives, and deepen my involvement in service. I hope to combine scientific knowledge with a community-centered perspective to help advance accessible, equitable, and compassionate healthcare. I am excited to continue growing as a student, leader, and future healthcare professional while exploring the many ways I can contribute to the well-being of others.
